**CRM** stands for **Customer Relationship Management**.

CRM is a system or software that helps businesses manage their relationships and interactions with customers, from the first contact to sales, support, and long-term customer retention.

In simple words, CRM helps a company keep all its customer information and communication in one place.

## **1. What does a CRM system do?**

Imagine you're working at a company that sells software to other businesses.

You might have hundreds of customers and potential customers. You need to know:

- Who are our customers?
    
- What products have they purchased?
    
- When did we last contact them?
    
- What problems have they reported?
    
- Are they interested in buying something?
    
- Who in our company is responsible for each customer?
    

A CRM system organizes all this information.

## **2. Main features of CRM**

|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|Contact Management|Stores customer names, emails, phone numbers, etc.|
|Sales Management|Tracks potential deals and sales progress|
|Lead Management|Manages potential customers (leads)|
|Customer Support|Tracks customer issues and support tickets|
|Task Management|Schedules calls, meetings, and follow-ups|
|Reporting|Analyzes sales and customer activity|

## **3. Example of how CRM works**

Imagine a customer visits your company's website and requests a demo.

The CRM workflow might look like this:

**1. Customer visits website**

Submits a demo request

**2. CRM creates a Lead**

Stores customer information

**3. Sales team contacts customer**

Records calls and meetings

**4. Deal is tracked**

Negotiation → Proposal → Closed

**5. Customer becomes a client**

Purchase and support history are maintained

## **5. CRM from a software development perspective**

If you're a developer working on a CRM project, you might encounter these concepts:

- **Frontend:** Customer dashboards, contact lists, sales pipelines.
    
- **Backend:** Business logic for customers, leads, deals, and users.
    
- **Database:** Stores customers, interactions, transactions, and activities.
    
- **Authentication & Authorization:** Controls which employees can access or modify customer data.
    
- **APIs:** Allow other systems to communicate with the CRM.
    
- **Automation:** Automatically assigns leads, sends notifications, or schedules follow-ups.
    

For example, a simplified CRM database might have these tables:

```
users
customers
leads
deals
activities
tasks
support_tickets
```

**One important distinction:** CRM isn't just a customer database. It's a complete business system for managing the customer lifecycle.

If you're onboarding onto a software project, understanding CRM concepts will help you understand why features such as customers, leads, deals, activities, and sales pipelines exist.
